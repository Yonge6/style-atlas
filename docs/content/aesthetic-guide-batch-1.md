## Cultural Safety Decisions

- `Aboriginal Dot Painting` remains the existing product style name and `styleId`; the guide itself uses `Australian First Nations art`, identifies cultural ownership, and explicitly rejects contextless copying.
- Everyday sections for culturally specific traditions ask users to observe rhythm, material, density, format and viewing relationships. They do not instruct users to reproduce sacred, ritual, courtly or community-restricted motifs.
- `Indian Miniature` is described as a field containing Mughal, Rajasthani, Pahari and other contexts, not one unified Indian appearance.
- `Madhubani` is paired with the term `Mithila` and described through varied community, wall, paper, ritual and contemporary practices.
- `Nihonga` is framed as a modern category that reorganizes inherited Japanese materials and formats, not as a synonym for mineral pigment.
- `Sumi-e`, Chinese ink painting, Gongbi and Shanshui are treated as related but non-interchangeable fields.
- Korean Minhwa and Chinese New Year woodblock guides avoid assigning one fixed meaning to every color, animal or object.
- No new prompts, commercial-use instructions, artist quotations or universal symbolic claims were added.
